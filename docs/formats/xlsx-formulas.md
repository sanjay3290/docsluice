# XLSX formulas

SpreadsheetML stores a formula in a cell's `<f>` element and its last cached result in `<v>`. docsluice reads the cached result as the cell's text and never evaluates the formula. With `formulas: true`, it also sets `Cell.formula` to the formula expression from `<f>`; with the option off, the formula field is omitted. This follows the XLS-4 requirement and the OOXML formula/cell model in [ECMA-376, Part 1](https://ecma-international.org/publications-and-standards/standards/ecma-376/).

A formula cell with no cached `<v>` has empty cell text and causes one `UNREADABLE_PART` warning for that worksheet. The warning is emitted whether or not formula text was requested. Warning messages do not include formula text or other cell contents. Formula source text is bounded to 2,000,000 characters per formula. Shared formula association is bounded to 100,000 distinct shared indexes (including invalid or ambiguous groups) and 20,000,000 combined characters of valid shared master formulas per worksheet. With formula output enabled, each returned formula string is checked and charged once against the caller's output-character budget, separately from the cell's cached text.

Shared formula masters are associated by their shared index in a worksheet-level two-pass lookup, so a dependent may appear before its master. A valid master must be the top-left cell of its declared `ref` range, and a dependent must lie within that range. Invalid or duplicate masters poison their shared index; dependents with missing or ambiguous groups are omitted and produce one generic association `UNREADABLE_PART` warning per worksheet. A master formula beyond the source text cap produces the bounded-capacity warning and also poisons its index. Dependents receive the master's formula text as written; relative references are not shifted. This is intentionally a text-only representation, not an expanded formula. Array formula text is returned as written on its formula cell.

The reader registers formulas during a source-only pass. It calls `resolve` once for each formula cell that it will retain in output, and skips calls for rejected cells. A successful `resolve` with formula output enabled charges that cell's returned formula text against `outputChars`; with formula output disabled it returns no formula and charges nothing. This avoids charging speculative formulas for cells that are later discarded.

## Integration limit

Formula characters are currently charged by the reader before the shared builder applies a caller-supplied transform. If that transform drops a table or strips or changes a formula, the counter can retain the pre-transform formula charge. Final acceptance requires the lead-owned builder to count retained formula text after transformation, followed by removal of the reader charge. The proposed regression cases and shared-core change are recorded in the package handoff; cached values are never evaluated.
